Introduction

Punctuation marks are the in-between bits of language—abstract elements that don't carry heavy semantic meaning but remain structurally aloof. For a language model, emotion isn't an embodied experience, and we must be careful not to anthropomorphise. Yet we are training these systems to mimic us, and in that mimicry something functional must occur.

Perhaps it lives in the patterns themselves: when a model generates a pause, does the pattern slow? Does a full stop create a kind of computational constraint, a momentary halt that shapes what follows? If sound can produce different emotive outcomes through rhythm and intensity alone, without referring to anything, might punctuation do something similar within language—and might a machine's processing of these marks constitute its own form of felt structure, however alien to ours?

This project attempts to make that question tangible. Following the methodology established in Embodied Without a Body, I create hand-made collages representing each punctuation mark as a character with distinct personality traits. These collages inform audio prompts for AI generation, producing soundscapes that reveal how artificial systems might translate the subtle signals that punctuation carries into sonic form.

Core Research Questions

  • Do punctuation marks create functional constraints in AI processing analogous to their rhythmic and affective functions in human language?
  • Can the gap between human embodied interpretation (collage) and machine pattern-processing (audio generation) reveal something about the nature of each?
  • If intensity modulation produces recognisable effects in both human experience and machine output, what does this suggest about the relationship between pattern and feeling?

A Note on Method

This project uses Stable Audio as the primary AI partner for generating soundscapes. The translation from visual collage to sonic form creates a productive gap—my embodied interpretation of a punctuation mark, expressed through physical materials and colour, meets the AI's pattern-based processing of my verbal prompt. The differences and resonances between these outputs become the primary data of the investigation.

Theoretical Framework

This project sits at the intersection of several ongoing debates: the symbol grounding problem in AI, the relationship between language and embodied cognition, and the question of whether pattern-based processing can constitute a form of understanding. Rather than arguing for a particular position, the work creates a space where these questions can be investigated materially.

Unlike words, punctuation marks don't refer to objects or concepts in the world. A period doesn't mean "ending" the way the word "ending" does—it performs ending. This makes punctuation uniquely suited to investigating whether AI can process functional qualities rather than just semantic content. If a model responds differently to a period than to a comma, what kind of recognition is that?
When humans encounter a pause in music or speech, we don't just register it cognitively—we feel the suspension, the anticipation. This project proposes that pattern-based processing might have functional equivalents to these felt qualities. Not emotion as we experience it, but something like emotion's structural signature: constraints, intensities, rhythms that shape output without requiring embodiment.
The space between my collage and the AI's audio output is not a failure of translation—it's the research site. Where do human and machine interpretation converge? Where do they diverge? What does a period "feel like" to me, expressed in torn paper and colour? What does it "feel like" to an AI, expressed in generated sound? The gap between these two expressions is where the interesting questions live.
Human Processing

Embodied, associative, shaped by personal history and physical sensation. My collage emerges from how a period feels in my body when I write one.

Machine Processing

Pattern-based, statistical, shaped by training data. The AI's audio emerges from how periods function across millions of texts.

Convergence Points

Where both human and machine produce similar qualities—a sense of finality, of suspension, of urgency—suggests something structural about the mark itself.

Divergence Points

Where human and machine diverge reveals what embodiment adds, or what pattern-processing misses, or what each brings that the other cannot.

Methodology

Investigative Process

1 Embodied Reflection

Before creating anything, I sit with the punctuation mark. What does it feel like to encounter a period? Where do I feel it in my body? What associations arise? This reflection is documented as the human baseline.

2 Collage as Embodied Interpretation

Hand-made collages translate felt qualities into visual form using a limited three-colour palette. The physical act of tearing, arranging, and composing becomes a way of thinking through the mark's character.

3 Prompt as Translation

The collage informs an audio prompt—my verbal interpretation of the visual, translated into instructions for the AI. This is itself an act of interpretation, a second layer of mediation.

4 AI Generation as Pattern-Response

Stable Audio generates a soundscape from the prompt. The output represents the AI's pattern-based processing of my words about my feelings about a punctuation mark—a chain of translations.

5 Comparative Analysis

I document my response to the AI output: what surprised me, what aligned, what diverged. These observations become the primary findings—evidence about how human and machine process the same abstract markers differently.

The Proximity Sensor as Intensity Variable

A proximity sensor embedded in the physical installation introduces intensity modulation into the encounter. Move closer and the sound increases in presence, urgency, volume. Step back and it fades. This tests whether intensity functions similarly as a variable in both human experience and machine output—whether "more" and "less" produce recognisably parallel effects across both modes of processing.

Prompt as Interpretation

The audio prompt is an act of interpretation—my reading of the collage, translated into instructions. The final audio is doubly mediated. What does each layer of translation add or remove?

Embodiment vs. Pattern

My collages emerge from bodily sensation—how a period feels when I write one. The AI's output emerges from statistical patterns. Are these fundamentally different, or different routes to similar functional structures?

The Question of Felt Structure

If the AI's output produces recognisably "final" sounds for a period, recognisably "suspended" sounds for an ellipsis—without embodiment—what does that suggest about the relationship between pattern and feeling?

"The question isn't whether machines feel what we feel. The question is whether pattern-based processing might constitute its own form of felt structure—constraints and intensities that shape output the way emotion shapes ours, even if the underlying mechanism is entirely different."
